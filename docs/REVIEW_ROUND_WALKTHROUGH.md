# Arcyn Find — reviewer walkthrough

Thanks for taking the time. Arcyn Find is a directory for finding AI tools:
you search or browse, compare what comes back, and save what's worth keeping.

This build is a private release. Please don't share the link.

Budget roughly **45–60 minutes**. Going in order matters — later sections
assume you have an account and some saved tools from earlier ones.

---

## How to report something

There's a **Feedback** button in the bottom-right corner of every page. It
attaches the page you're on and your browser details automatically, so you
don't need to describe where you were.

Use it the moment you notice something, rather than saving it all up. A note
written in the moment is worth more than a summary written an hour later.

What makes a report easy to act on:

- **What you expected** — "I thought clicking the logo would take me home"
- **What happened instead** — "it reloaded the same page"
- **How much it bothered you** — blocked / annoying / barely worth mentioning

That last one matters more than it sounds. We can fix almost anything, but not
everything at once, and we can't tell severity from the report alone.

Reactions are as useful as bugs. "This page felt slow", "I didn't know what
this button did", "I expected pricing here" are all worth sending.

## Before you start

- Use whichever device you'd normally use. If you have both, do the first pass
  on desktop and the mobile pass at the end (§10).
- You'll need a Google account to sign in — there's no email/password option.
- If you hit something that stops you completely, send it and skip ahead.

---

## 1. First impressions — the landing page

Open the site. **Before you click anything**, wait about ten seconds.

- What do you think this site is for? Say it in your own words.
- Who do you think it's for?
- What's the first thing you want to click?

Then send that as your first piece of feedback, even if it seems obvious. First
impressions are the one thing you can only give us once.

## 2. Browsing without an account

Stay signed out.

- Browse the tool listings. Try the filters and sorting.
- Open a few categories. Do the tools inside match the category name?
- Does anything look duplicated or out of place? (See §11 first.)
- Try to find a tool for a task you actually have. Did you find one?

## 3. A tool's own page

Open any tool that interests you.

- Is there enough here to decide whether to try it?
- What's missing that you'd want before clicking through?
- Check the pricing, if shown. Does it match the tool's own site?
- Try the related tools. Are they genuinely related?

## 4. Search

Search is the main way in, so push on it.

- Search for a tool you already know by name. Is it the top result?
- Search by what you want to *do* — "summarise long documents", "remove image
  backgrounds" — rather than by name. Are the results sensible?
- Try a deliberate typo.
- Try something the site almost certainly doesn't have.
- Try a very broad word like "video".

For each: were the results useful, and did they arrive fast enough?

## 5. Signing up

Create an account with Google.

- Was anything about the sign-in confusing or alarming?
- Did you end up where you expected afterwards?
- Go through onboarding. Were the questions clear? Did the answers seem to
  affect what you were shown afterwards?

Note anything that made you hesitate. Hesitation at sign-up is expensive.

## 6. Signed in

- Look at your home page. Is it different from the signed-out landing page in a
  way that's useful?
- Are any recommendations relevant to what you said during onboarding?

## 7. Saving and collecting

- Save a few tools.
- Make a collection and add tools to it.
- Edit the collection — rename it, remove something.
- Leave and come back. Is everything still there?

## 8. Reviews and community

- Write a review of a tool you've actually used.
- Find someone else's review. Mark one helpful.
- Look at the community area and another person's profile.
- Try following someone, then unfollowing.

## 9. Settings

- Open settings and change something — a notification toggle, a privacy option.
- **Save it, reload the page, and check it stuck.** This is worth being fussy
  about; tell us if any switch forgets what you set.
- Update your profile — display name, avatar.
- Switch between light and dark mode. Look for anything unreadable.

## 10. Mobile pass

Open the site on your phone and repeat §1, §2, §4 and §7 quickly.

- Is anything cut off, overlapping or too small to tap?
- Does the menu work the way you'd expect?
- Is any text uncomfortable to read?

## 11. Things we already know about

Please **don't spend reports on these** — they're known and already queued:

- **Duplicate tools.** The underlying dataset contains a lot of duplicate
  entries for the same product. Search filters them out, but browsing and
  category pages can still show them.
- **Wrong categories.** A small share of tools sit in a category that's plainly
  wrong. Known and being worked on. *Do* tell us if a search result is wrong —
  that's different and useful.
- **Missing or odd pricing.** Pricing is scraped and often incomplete. Where an
  annual plan exists, it's shown as its monthly equivalent, so a "$6/year" plan
  appears as $0.50/mo. Deliberate, but say so if it reads as misleading.
- **Search quality drops for obscure tools.** Well-known tools are indexed far
  more richly than long-tail ones.
- **Trending may look thin or stale.** One of its data sources is currently
  failing. Being fixed separately.
- **No weekly digest.** It isn't built yet, so there's no setting for it.

## 12. Last questions

When you're done, send these as one final piece of feedback:

1. Would you use this? Honestly — a "no" is more useful than a polite "yes".
2. What one thing would most make you come back?
3. What was the single most annoying moment?
4. If you already use something else for this, what does it do better?

Thanks again.
